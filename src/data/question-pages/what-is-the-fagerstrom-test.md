      "heading": "What the test is trying to measure",
      "paragraphs": [
        "Nicotine dependence is not the same as smoking a lot. Two people can smoke the same number of cigarettes and have very different relationships with nicotine: one can go hours without thinking about it, the other wakes at night to smoke. The Fagerström Test for Nicotine Dependence tries to capture that difference by asking about the timing and compulsiveness of smoking, not just the daily count.",
        "It was developed as a short, practical questionnaire for clinics and research. The original Fagerström Tolerance Questionnaire was published in 1978; the revised six-item version, the FTND, was published by Heatherton and colleagues in 1991. It remains one of the most widely used dependence scales, though it is not the only one."

      "heading": "The six questions",
      "paragraphs": [
        "The FTND asks: how soon after waking do you smoke your first cigarette? Do you find it difficult to refrain from smoking where it is forbidden? Which cigarette would you hate most to give up — the first in the morning or another one? How many cigarettes a day do you smoke? Do you smoke more in the first hours after waking than during the rest of the day? Do you smoke even when you are so ill that you are in bed most of the day?",
        "Each answer is scored, and the six scores are added. The first and fourth questions carry more weight because they have more response options; the others are yes-or-no items. That is why the total runs from 0 to 10 rather than 0 to 6. You can find copies of the questionnaire online, but the version you use should be the standard one, not a modified quiz from a commercial site."

      "paragraphs": [

      "paragraphs": [
        "The most common misreading is that a high score means you cannot quit, or that a low score means you do not need help. Neither follows. The score describes the strength of dependence on average; it does not describe your motivation, your support, your health or your previous experience. A high score is information about the biology and the habit, not a judgment about you.
