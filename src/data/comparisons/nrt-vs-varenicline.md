Nicotine replacement and varenicline are the two pharmacological routes most people are offered first, and they work in different ways. [NRT](/medications/) supplies nicotine without the smoke, so the body gets the drug it is withdrawing from while the cigarette habit is dismantled. [Varenicline](/medications/varenicline/) changes what the nicotine receptor does: it produces a partial signal and blocks most of the reward a cigarette would deliver.

Both are first-line treatments in major guidelines. The useful question is not which one wins in the abstract. It is which one wins against the version of NRT you would actually use, and which routine you would actually keep up for the weeks that decide the attempt.

## At a glance

| Question | NRT | Varenicline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Nicotine delivered without tobacco smoke | A prescription partial agonist at the nicotinic receptor |
| Forms | Patch, gum, lozenge, inhalator, mouth spray | Tablet course |
| How it is used | Daily baseline and/or on-demand doses | Fixed schedule set by the prescriber |
| Evidence vs the other | Lower than varenicline as a single form; equal to varenicline as combination NRT | Higher than single-form NRT; no clear difference against combination NRT |
| Routine burden | Product in reach; technique matters | Remembering a tablet schedule |
| Common side effects | Skin irritation, hiccups, jaw ache, throat irritation | Nausea, abnormal dreams, insomnia |
| Access | Over the counter in many countries | Prescription only |
| Cost | Lower for generics | Usually higher without subsidy |
| Best fit | People who want control, no prescription, or a product they can stop | People who want a defined course and can tolerate the side effects |

## What the trials actually compared

The headline that "varenicline works better than NRT" comes from comparisons that mostly used a single nicotine replacement product, usually the patch. That distinction matters, because combination NRT — a patch plus a fast-acting form — is a different treatment from a patch alone.

Cochrane's 2023 review of nicotine receptor partial agonists pooled the direct comparisons. Against a single form of NRT, varenicline produced higher quit rates: risk ratio 1.25 (95% CI 1.14 to 1.37), high-certainty evidence, eleven studies and 7,572 participants. Against dual-form NRT, the pooled result was a risk ratio of 1.02 (0.87 to 1.20), low-certainty evidence, five studies and 2,344 participants. In plain terms: varenicline clearly beat one NRT product, and did not clearly beat two used together.

| Comparison | Result | Certainty | Evidence base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Varenicline vs placebo | RR 2.32 (2.15–2.51) | High | 41 studies, 17,395 participants |
| Varenicline vs single-form NRT | RR 1.25 (1.14–1.37) | High | 11 studies, 7,572 participants |
| Varenicline vs dual-form NRT | RR 1.02 (0.87–1.20) | Low | 5 studies, 2,344 participants |
| Any NRT vs placebo or no medication | Quit rates roughly 50–70% higher | High | Cochrane NRT review |
| Varenicline vs nicotine patch (EAGLES) | Odds ratio 1.68 (1.46–1.93) | One large RCT | 8,144 participants |

EAGLES is worth separating from the meta-analysis because it is the largest single trial and it randomised people to varenicline, bupropion, nicotine patch or placebo, all with brief counselling. Varenicline beat the patch on biochemically confirmed abstinence for weeks 9 to 12, with an odds ratio of 1.68. That is a direct comparison, not an indirect one, and it points the same way as the Cochrane pooled estimate.

The honest summary is narrower than a ranking: varenicline is more effective than one NRT product; it is not established as more effective than two used together. Anyone who has read that varenicline is "the strongest" and expects a large margin over well-used combination NRT is working from a comparison that was never made.

## Why the answer changes with the NRT you compare

A [patch](/medications/nicotine-patch/) alone gives a steady background level. It does not answer a craving that spikes in the three minutes after a coffee. Gum, lozenge, inhalator and spray answer spikes but do not hold a baseline through the day. Most heavier smokers have both a background problem and a spike problem, which is why combination NRT outperforms a single form.

That is the fair fight for varenicline, and it is the one where the difference disappears. The practical implication is not that the two are interchangeable. It is that "NRT" is not one treatment, and a comparison that treats it as one will mislead you.

The [combination NRT comparison](/comparisons/combination-nrt-vs-single-form/) sets out the size of the step from one product to two. The [patch versus gum versus lozenge comparison](/comparisons/nicotine-patch-vs-gum-vs-lozenge/) helps match a single form to the shape of your cravings. And the [NRT versus no medication comparison](/comparisons/nrt-vs-no-medication/) puts the class effect in context: any NRT is clearly better than nothing, which is the finding both treatments share.

## How they feel different day to day

The patch is invisible once it is on. People describe a flat baseline — less irritability, less of the constant pull — and then a sharp spike that the patch does not touch. That spike is the argument for keeping gum or lozenge within reach even while using a patch.

Varenicline is felt more as a change in the cigarette than in the day. Community reports commonly describe cigarettes becoming pointless: the first one of the morning does not deliver what it used to, and the reward that drove the habit is muted. That effect is the mechanism working. It does not remove the habit, the cues or the oral urge, which is why behavioural support still matters.

The routines differ in a way that decides adherence for some people. NRT asks you to carry something and use it at the right moment; technique is part of the treatment. Varenicline asks you to remember a tablet on a schedule and to keep taking it when you feel nauseated. One routine suits people who want control in their own hands. The other suits people who would rather not negotiate with a craving every few hours.

## Side effects and safety

Nausea is the most frequently reported varenicline side effect. In EAGLES it was reported by about a quarter of participants on varenicline, and abnormal dreams and insomnia were also common. Most reports describe these as early and manageable, but "manageable" is a group average; some people stop the course because of them, and that is a legitimate outcome to discuss with a prescriber rather than endure silently.

NRT's side effects are local and product-specific: skin irritation from patches, hiccups and jaw ache from gum, throat irritation from inhalators and sprays. They are usually addressed by changing technique or rotating sites rather than by stopping treatment.

On serious adverse events, the reviews are more cautious than the headlines. Cochrane found moderate-certainty evidence that people taking varenicline are more likely to report a serious adverse event than people not taking it (RR 1.23, 1.01 to 1.48), while the comparison against NRT suggested fewer reported serious events (RR 0.70, 0.50 to 0.99, low certainty). The confidence intervals are wide and the certainty is limited, which is why the fair reading is "uncommon, and worth a conversation with a prescriber" rather than either "safe" or "dangerous".

The neuropsychiatric story is the one most people have heard about. EAGLES was designed to test it in 8,144 smokers, half with a psychiatric diagnosis, and did not find a significant increase in moderate-to-severe neuropsychiatric events attributable to varenicline or bupropion compared with patch or placebo. Regulators subsequently removed the boxed neuropsychiatric warning. That does not mean mood changes never happen; it means the earlier signal was smaller than feared, and any mood change should still be reported promptly.

Pregnancy is a separate conversation. USPSTF recommends behavioural support for pregnant people and finds the evidence insufficient to assess pharmacotherapy; NICE and NHS guidance treat NRT as an option under professional guidance rather than a first-line self-started treatment. Varenicline is not the usual choice in pregnancy. The [pregnancy population page](/populations/pregnancy/) covers that in more detail, and the decision belongs to a clinician.

## Cost, prescription and access

NRT is available without a prescription in many countries, which makes it the lower-friction option. Generic patches and generic gum are substantially cheaper than branded products, and national quitlines or health services in some countries supply NRT at reduced cost or free.

Varenicline requires a prescriber, which adds an appointment and, in many health systems, a cost. Insurance coverage varies, and availability varies by country. That access difference is not a detail: a treatment you cannot obtain is not a treatment option, and a cheaper course completed beats a more effective course abandoned.

Cost also interacts with adherence in a way people underestimate. NRT users who ration gum to make a pack last are not really using the treatment. Varenicline users who stop at the first week of nausea have paid for a course they did not finish. In both cases the failure is the regimen, not the molecule.

## Who tends to do better on which

| If this describes you | Worth discussing first | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You want no prescription and control over timing | NRT, ideally combination | Over the counter, adjustable, and a fast-acting form answers spikes |
| A previous patch-only attempt stalled | Varenicline, or combination NRT | The uncovered craving shape is the problem, not your motivation |
| You have had nausea or vivid dreams on varenicline | NRT | Tolerability decides adherence more than average efficacy |
| You want a defined course with a start and end | Varenicline | A tablet schedule is simpler than managing two products |
| You have kidney impairment or another condition your prescriber is monitoring | NRT, or varenicline with review | Suitability is a clinical decision, not a website one |
| You are pregnant or breastfeeding | Behavioural support first; clinician decision on NRT | Pharmacotherapy evidence in pregnancy is limited |
| Cost is the binding constraint | Generic NRT | The cheapest option you will use consistently wins |
| You have both all-day withdrawal and sharp spikes | Combination NRT or varenicline | Both address the two shapes; pick on access and tolerance |

This is a decision table, not a prescribing table. It contains no doses, no schedules and no tapering instructions, because those depend on the product, the person and the clinical picture.

## Can you use both?

Combining NRT with varenicline is done in some clinical settings, and it is not a DIY arrangement. It means two sources of nicotine effect at once, with additive side effects and no strong trial base for the specific combination in most populations. If you have read about it and want to try it, the request belongs with the prescriber who is managing the varenicline, not with a supplement stack assembled from forum posts.

The same applies to using NRT while still smoking. That is discouraged for good reason — it adds a rapid nicotine hit on top of a replacement level and keeps the cigarette cue active. The [question on smoking while using NRT](/questions/can-i-smoke-while-using-nrt/) covers what happens and what to do after a slip.

## Common mistakes

**Comparing varenicline to the weakest version of NRT.** A patch alone is not the best NRT. If you are choosing between treatments, compare varenicline with combination NRT, which is the comparison the trials actually ran.

**Stopping varenicline at the first wave of nausea.** Nausea is common and often settles, but it is also a reason to talk to the prescriber rather than to stop alone. Abrupt discontinuation and an unmanaged side effect produce the same result: an unfinished course.

**Rationing NRT.** Buying one pack of gum and using it sparingly through the second week is a plan for white-knuckling, not a plan for treatment. The fast-acting form is the part that handles the moments.

**Expecting either to replace support.** Both treatments roughly double or better the odds of quitting on average. Neither teaches you what to do at 9pm on a Friday. The [quit-smoking guide](/quit-smoking/) and the [cravings guide](/cravings/) cover the behavioural half.

**Treating a lapse as a verdict.** A cigarette on day nine is data about which situation was uncovered. It is not evidence that the medication failed. The [relapse section](/relapse/) is built around that distinction.

**Starting without a plan for the first 72 hours.** Whichever route you choose, the first three days are where technique and preparation matter most. The [withdrawal timeline](/withdrawal/timeline/) shows what to expect hour by hour.

## A seven-day decision plan

| Day | Action | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Write down your craving pattern: all-day, situational, or both | Does your choice match the shape of your day? |
| 2 | Check access and cost for both routes where you live | Is one route realistically obtainable this month? |
| 3 | If varenicline is an option, book the prescriber appointment | Can you discuss kidney function, mood history and side effects? |
| 4 | If NRT, decide single or combination and buy enough for two weeks | Will you run out before the hardest week ends? |
| 5 | Read the product labels and ask a pharmacist about technique | Do you know how to use it properly, not just how to buy it? |
| 6 | Set a quit date and arrange behavioural support | Is there a person, service or plan for the first week? |
| 7 | Review: one route, one routine, one support contact | Can you explain your plan in one sentence? |

A week is enough to choose a route and remove the practical obstacles. It is not enough to judge whether the route will work; that judgement belongs to weeks, and to whether the treatment was used as intended.

## What this page cannot tell you

It cannot tell you which treatment to take, what dose to use, how long to continue, or whether either option is safe for your medical history. Those decisions belong to a prescriber or pharmacist with your history in front of them. The evidence summarised here is population-level, current to the sources listed below, and does not predict any individual outcome. This page has not been reviewed by a clinician.

The practical summary is shorter than the table: varenicline beats one NRT product, combination NRT matches varenicline, and access plus tolerability decide more attempts than the ranking does. Pick the route you can obtain, tolerate and finish — then spend your planning energy on the first week rather than on the choice.
